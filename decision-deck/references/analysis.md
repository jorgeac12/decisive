# Analysis before slides

Deliver the items below in Markdown, in this order. Skip an item only if it makes no sense for the deck type, and say that you skipped it.

## 1. Thesis in one sentence
What the material solves or communicates, written for the audience, without jargon.

## 2. Map
Components, steps, workstreams or options, in the order the data, process or argument flows. For each: name, role in one line, stated owner (or "no stated owner").

## 3. Decided
Everything the text treats as settled. Only what is written.

## 4. Open, as stated
Everything the text itself marks as pending, under discussion, "to be defined", "open point".

## 5. Not addressed (the most valuable part)
Look actively and list what you find:
- **Diffuse ownership:** "shared", "joint", "between teams", "everyone".
- **Rule without a mechanism:** a ban or standard that does not appear in permissions, automation, tests or a review process.
- **Undefined time:** missing deadline, SLA, frequency, window, freshness or retention.
- **Undefined success:** a pilot, POC or goal without measurable exit criteria.
- **Cost to run:** the text covers the cost to build and forgets the cost to operate.
- **Edge cases:** what arrives late, what gets corrected later, what must be deleted, what happens on failure.
- **External obligation:** regulation, privacy, audit or contract mentioned in passing.
- **People and capacity:** who executes, with what time, and what stops being done.

## 6. Inconsistencies between documents
Compare the files with each other, with attention to permission tables, deadlines, numbers, component names and responsibilities. For each contradiction: what A says, what B says, what must be decided. With a single document, look for internal contradictions (section against section, diagram against text).

## 7. Claims that are too strong
Absolute statements ("100%", "never", "always", "guarantees", "eliminates", "zero") that would not survive a skeptical executive. For each: what would need validation before saying it.

## 8. Risks by impact
At most 6. Columns: risk, business impact (not technical), level (High, Medium, Low), suggested mitigation. Sort by level. Notice whether the high risks are engineering problems or decision problems: that is often the message of the deck.

## 9. Decisions or actions to request
3 to 5 concrete items, each starting with an imperative verb (Name, Approve, Define, Choose, Prioritize, Fund). They must come from items 5 to 8, not from nowhere.

## 10. Numbers quoted
Table: number, where it appears, measured or estimated. Estimates get the validation label in the deck.

## 11. Data reading
For each set of numbers that could become a visual:
- **Question it answers:** trend, ranking, target, bridge, mix or schedule.
- **Candidate chart** (see `charts.md`) and what gets highlighted.
- **Available comparison:** against target, previous period or benchmark. Without one, say the number is loose.
- **Observation, implication, recommendation,** one line each.
- **Gap:** the number that would be needed for the conclusion to stand (becomes a placeholder or a data request).

If the documents have no numbers, say so. Never fabricate a series to have a chart; use `big-numbers`, `kpis` with placeholders, or text components.

## 12. Big Idea and storyboard
- **Big Idea** in one sentence, with a point of view and what is at stake.
- **SCQA** for the executive summary (situation, complication, question, answer).
- **Storyboard:** the list of action titles for every slide, in order, with the component of each. Read in sequence, the titles tell the whole story (horizontal logic).

## 13. Placeholders
List of what is missing and you cannot invent.
